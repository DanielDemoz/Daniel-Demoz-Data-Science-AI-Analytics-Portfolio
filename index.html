About

Governance that comes from building

I focus on AI governance. I help Canadian organizations choose AI vendors, write policy, assess risk, and train their staff. The aim is simple: people can explain what an AI system does, check that it works, and stay accountable for the result.

I also build. At the University of Ottawa, I led a team of four that resolved 23 million public funding records into about 851,000 organizations. I wrote the Splink matching stage, the language model step, and the validation. Every flag links to its source, and we present results as signals, not proof.

My research looks at how oversight works in practice. One paper studies human oversight and automation bias in Canada's Directive on Automated Decision-Making. Another reviews AI governance maturity across Ontario's 24 public colleges.

I teach AI governance at Seneca Polytechnic. I have also taught machine learning, neural networks, and big data analytics at Centennial College and data management and analytics at George Brown. I hold a Master of Interdisciplinary Artificial Intelligence from the University of Ottawa, and I am working toward the IAPP AI Governance Professional (AIGP) certification.

University of Ottawa · AI For Accountability Hackathon · April 2026

Public Funding Accountability Platform

I led a team of four on a platform that resolves duplicate organizations across Canadian public funding and charity data. About 23 million records resolved into about 851,000 organizations. Matching runs in stages: direct matching, probability scoring with Splink, a language model step, then validation and human review.

Governance is part of the design. Outputs are anonymized, every flag links back to its source, and findings are presented as signals, not proof.
