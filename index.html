Prototype · synthetic data

Student risk flags for an Ontario college

Advisors needed an early, simple way to see which students may be falling behind, using grade and attendance data the college already had.

I built a prototype web tool that reads one Excel sheet of grades and attendance. A student is flagged if grade or attendance is under 70%, and placed in one of four groups: attendance concern, grade concern, both, or no concern. The tool drafts an email for the advisor to review and send. I presented it to the college's leadership using synthetic data only. It never touched real student records.

Governance design

  • A rule people can explain. A machine learning model runs only when past outcome data exists to test it.
  • A person decides. The tool drafts emails. It never contacts a student.
  • Only the data it needs. Student ID, name, program, grade, and attendance.
  • Tested scoring. Unit tests check the scoring rule and the Excel reading.

Risks and controls before any real use

RiskControl
A single 70% cut-off misses a student at 71% who is dropping fastShow the trend over the term, not one number
Attendance can reflect work or family duties, not effortCompare flag rates across student groups before launch, and review them each term
Student records are personal informationLimit access by role, set a retention period, and log who views results
Staff treat a flag as a verdictLabel each flag "a reason to check in" and record the advisor's decision
Wrong data in the uploadCheck ranges and missing values on upload, and reject bad files

What applies: law

  • FIPPA (Ontario) for a public college: collection, use, disclosure, and retention of student personal information.
  • PIPEDA for a private career college handling personal information in its business.
  • Ontario Human Rights Code: flags that fall harder on a protected group could be discrimination in a service, even without intent.
  • Enhancing Digital Security and Trust Act, 2024: sets rules for AI use by public institutions. Its AI requirements wait on regulations not yet in place.

What applies: voluntary guidance

  • IPC and OHRC joint AI principles (January 2026): valid and reliable, safe, privacy protective, human rights affirming, transparent, accountable. Not binding, but both regulators say they will use them to assess public institutions' AI.
  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage.

Limits: this is a prototype built on synthetic data. It was not tested against real student outcomes, so the 70% cut-off and the risk groups are untested.

My method

The Reliable AI Adoption Framework.

I developed this framework to answer six questions before any AI tool is chosen. My book, Evidence-Based AI, sets out the evidence discipline behind it. Each question produces a written record, so every decision can be checked later.

01

Business problem

What outcome needs to improve, and how will we know it has?

OutputProblem statement and success measure
02

Data readiness

What data exists, where does it live, and is it reliable enough to base decisions on?

OutputData inventory and quality check
03

Governance and risk

Which risks and rules apply before anyone relies on the system?

OutputRisk assessment and the rules that apply, such as PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25, or OSFI E-23 for federally regulated financial institutions
04

Technology and tools

What is already in use, what works, and which vendor fits?

OutputVendor review and decision record
05

People and adoption

Who uses the system, who reviews its outputs, and what training do they need?

OutputOversight roles and training plan
06

Outcomes and ROI

What does success look like in numbers?

OutputBaseline measures, then reviews at 30 and 90 days

The framework aligns with the Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage functions of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework.

Typical governance records

AI use-case inventory · risk assessment · control matrix · vendor due-diligence checklist · model or system card · human oversight procedure · approval record · monitoring plan.

Evidence before assurance

Claims need records and tests behind them. A framework mapping or an assessment score alone does not prove that an AI system is safe, fair, lawful, or effective.
